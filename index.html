01Course summary

This class is a platform-agnostic introduction to the editorial and technical skills of photo and video. We'll use audio, photo, video, and words to tell stories — both news and features. Class exercises and field assignments give you room to practice and develop these skills. You'll get hands-on experience shooting photos with your smartphone, shooting video with the Canon C200, and editing in Adobe Premiere.

02What you'll learn

  • How visual storytelling differs from print and audio
  • The basics of news photography
  • Reporting and interviewing specifically for video
  • How to shoot high-quality video that tells a story
  • Clear, concise script writing
  • Adobe Premiere editing software
  • The many forms of visual journalism: news, documentary, explainer, social, and more

Get two hard drives — one working drive, one to back it up. There are only two kinds of hard drives: one that has just failed and one that is about to. A cloud drive works as your backup if you'll commit to the upload time. Always bring your drives and current footage to class.

04Diversity, equity & inclusion

I've deliberately chosen topics, examples, and assignments that ask you to engage with a diversity of voices, perspectives, and lived experiences. In this space we stay open to learning and growth and commit to treating each other with courtesy and respect.

As journalists, you'll be expected to consider people and viewpoints you don't agree with — I'll purposely bring controversial topics into class. During discussions, respect each other's unique lived experiences and points of view, aim to be thoughtfully critical of the work and not the person, and always be kind.

08Assignments & exercises

Two exercises get you comfortable with the tools, and two video assignments carry the semester. Each assignment moves through pitch → rough → final, and you'll fold my feedback on the rough into the final cut.

Exercises Shooting (the 5×5) and Editing — your first reps with the camera and with Premiere. Assignment 1 The "on-the-job" profile. Assignment 2 The final story. Pick any form — a documentary, a news piece, an explainer, a series of social videos (2–3), a combination of those, or something new.

During the photo unit (weeks 1–4) you'll also turn in a weekly photo assignment.

C200 camera settings

Shoot MP4 to the SD card, 1920 × 1080, 29.97P (“30p”).

Press MENU. Each menu has an icon and numbered pages. With the joystick, pick the menu, then the page number, then the item, and press the joystick to select. CANCEL goes back a level.

1 · Recording format

MenuSettingSet toWhy
Recording/Media Setup · p1System Frequency59.94 HzUS standard. Changing it restarts the camera.
Recording/Media Setup · p1Main Rec FormatMP4 (SD Card)Not RAW — RAW needs CFast cards and heavy post.
Recording/Media Setup · p2Frame Rate29.97PThis is “30p.”
Recording/Media Setup · p2Resolution/Color Sampling1920x1080 YCC420 8 bitNot 3840x2160 (4K).

Initialize the SD card in the camera before each shoot: Recording/Media Setup · p1 › Initialize Media › SD Card A. This erases the card — make sure last shoot’s footage is offloaded.

2 · Image, focus & controls

  • Shutter: 1/60. Camera Setup · p2 › Shutter Mode: Speed, then dial in 1/60.
  • No Canon Log. Custom Picture/HDR · p1 › Preset: Off. Log footage looks grey and flat and needs color grading.
  • Lens to MF. Flip the AF/MF switch on the side of the lens to MF.
  • Peaking on, red. Assistance Functions · p2 › Peaking: VIDEO Output On (and Peaking: VF Output On) › Peaking 1 › Color: Red.
  • Rule-of-thirds grid. Assistance Functions · p6 › Markers: VF+VIDEO Output On › Grid Marker: pick a color (e.g. White).
  • Dials. System Setup · p4 › Camera Grip Ctrl Dial: ISO/Gain; Camera Ctrl Dial (body): Iris.

3 · Audio

  • Both input switches on ANALOG (not AES/EBU — levels can’t be adjusted there).
  • INPUT 1 — on-camera shotgun mic. Source switch: MIC+48V. Plug the mic in before switching phantom power on.
  • INPUT 2 — wireless mic receiver. Source switch: MIC (no phantom power).
  • Set levels manually; peaks around −12 dB.
  • Wear headphones the whole time you’re rolling.

Before you roll

  • Battery charged, spare in the bag
  • SD card initialized, LOCK switch off
  • MP4 · 1920 × 1080 · 29.97P
  • Shutter 1/60, no Canon Log, lens on MF, peaking and grid on
  • Shotgun in INPUT 1 (+48V), wireless in INPUT 2, levels set, headphones on
  • Record a 10-second test and play it back

Setting up a Premiere project

Set up the folders and settings before you import a single clip. Everything lives on your external SSD, never the internal drive. Follows the coaches’ videos 1–3.

1 · Folder structure (on the SSD)

Make one blank template folder once, then duplicate it for every project. Name each copy YYMMDD_ProjectTitle (e.g. 261009_5x5).

YYMMDD_ProjectTitle/ ├── project files/ │ └── scratch disk/ ├── footage/ │ ├── interviews/ │ └── b-roll/ ├── audio/ ├── graphics/ ├── miscellaneous/ ├── exports/ └── scripts/

Copy the SD card into footage, sorting interviews from b-roll as you go.

2 · Create the project

  1. File › New › Project. Save it inside project files. Choose Skip import mode.
  2. Scratch disks: File › Project Settings › Scratch Disks — point every option at the scratch disk folder.
  3. Autosave: Premiere Pro › Settings › Auto Save — every 5 minutes, 20 versions. Still press Cmd+S often.
  4. Workspace: Window › Workspaces › Editing. If panels get scrambled: Reset to Saved Layout.

3 · Audio setting — before you import

The C200 records each mic on its own channel. Imported as stereo, the shotgun on channel 1 plays only in the left ear. Fix it once, before importing:

  1. Premiere Pro › Settings › Timeline (older versions: Audio) › Default Audio Tracks.
  2. Multichannel Mono Media: set to Mono.
  3. Click OK.

Now each mic lands on its own track (A1 shotgun, A2 wireless) and plays in both ears. Keep the one that sounds best; mute or delete the other.

Already imported? Right-click the clips › Modify › Audio Channels › Clip Channel Format: Mono. Or use the coaches’ fix: Effects › Fill Right with Left (video 5).

4 · Import and make your sequence

  1. Import: drag the footage and audio folders from Finder into the Project panel. Folders become bins automatically.
  2. New bin: Cmd+B, for anything extra (graphics, music).
  3. First sequence: drag your first clip onto the empty timeline.
  4. At the Clip Mismatch Warning, choose Change Sequence Settings so the sequence matches your 1080 30p footage.
  5. Rename the sequence (e.g. 5x5_v1) and save: Cmd+S.

Before you start editing

  • Project folder is on the SSD and named YYMMDD_ProjectTitle
  • Project file saved in project files; scratch disks point to scratch disk
  • Autosave on: 5 min, 20 versions
  • Multichannel mono set to Mono before importing
  • Sequence matches the footage (1920 × 1080, 29.97 fps)

Stuck? Ask the tech helper below or watch the coaches’ videos 1–3.

Exporting from Premiere

Export every assignment at 1920 × 1080, H.264 — roughly 120–300 MB for a 2-minute piece. Match Source copies your sequence’s frame size, so shoot in 1080 and the export comes out 1080. Footage shot in 4K exports at 4K — a 500 MB+ file that is slow to upload and slow to review.

Export, step by step

  1. Click the timeline so the sequence is selected, not a clip in the Project panel.
  2. File › Export › Export Media (Cmd+M).
  3. File name: YYMMDD_LastName_Assignment — e.g. 261009_Fox_5x5.
  4. Location: your project’s exports folder on the SSD.
  5. Format: H.264. Preset: Match Source – Adaptive Medium Bitrate.
  6. Do the two checks below, then click Export.
  7. Watch the exported file start to finish before you upload it.

12Plagiarism, fabrication & AI

Don't borrow the work of others and claim it as your own. Don't fabricate content and present it as truth. Don't use quotes without attributing them to their sources, and avoid using images and video without permission. If you do use material made by others with permission, credit the source — out of respect for the creators, and so your audience knows where it came from.

On AI: you may not use generative AI to produce reporting or writing you present as your own. Any permitted use of AI tools must be disclosed — we'll cover the ethics and disclosure norms in our AI in video session.

If you have any doubts about something you're considering, come see me before you cross to the dark side. I'm generally pro-AI and I'd love to see how you use it. But I'll have no patience for using AI in a way that the class believes is your own work without disclosing it. Better to figure out an ethically sound way to do something than to risk your credibility, reputation, and career. For the full Code of Ethics, see the Code of Ethics website.
